What it gets right

  • Density reads as competence. Showing the actual segment markers proves you parsed the container rather than guessing.
  • "Unmeasured" is native here. An instrument that lists its own range is normal; the honest limit looks like rigour rather than a gap.
  • Earns technical backlinks — the audience most likely to link to you is the one this flatters.

What it costs

  • Alienates the biggest search segment. Someone Googling "remove location from photo" is not comforted by APP1.
  • Dark-first hurts long-form reading, and the guides are the SEO engine.
  • Closest to the hacker aesthetic the brand rules exclude — needs a careful hand to stay clinical rather than illicit.

What it gets right

  • Built for the actual growth engine. Nine long guides are the acquisition strategy; this is the only direction designed for reading them.
  • "Not established" is the strongest phrasing of the limit — evidentiary language makes the caveat sound authoritative instead of apologetic.
  • Distinct in the category. Every competitor in this search space looks like a converter tool; none look like a reference.

What it costs

  • Risks feeling slow for someone who arrived to strip EXIF in ten seconds and does not want a publication.
  • Serif at small sizes needs discipline — the ledger rows are where it would degrade first.
  • Least obviously "a tool", which may soften the call to action.

What it gets right

  • Matches search intent. Tier-3 keywords — "remove metadata from image", "exif remover" — are typed by people who want an outcome, not a readout.
  • "We can't check" is the most honest phrasing of all three because it is the one a non-expert cannot misread.
  • Highest completion rate. A verdict then a button is the shortest path from landing to download.

What it costs

  • Sacrifices the technical audience who would otherwise link to you, unless the disclosure panel is genuinely deep.
  • Softness can read as unserious — the one direction where "friendly" risks tipping into the scammy-converter look the brand rules forbid.
  • Most generic. Rounded cards and an indigo accent are the current default; it needs sharper detailing to feel authored.

Recommendation

Dossier, with Plainspoken's verdict

Lead with authority, then answer the question in plain words

The business case for this site is search: nine long guides feeding fourteen tool pages, earning links that compound. Only Dossier is designed for the reading half of that, and it is the only one of the three that does not look like every other utility in the category — which matters more for links than for conversion.

Its single weakness is the impatient visitor, and that is cheap to fix: put Plainspoken's verdict block at the top of the results, then let the ruled record sit underneath it. You get the plain-language answer for the person who wants it and the full evidence for the person who links to you, without maintaining two designs.

Instrument is the one I would not build as the whole site — but its readout is worth keeping as the expanded state behind "show technical detail", where its density is an asset rather than a barrier.
